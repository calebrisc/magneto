What the lab is for, per build

The lab's machines, sorted by whether they actually move each build forward. Crucial is the reason to join · Middle is useful but has alternatives · anything not listed doesn't touch the build. Training classes are required before touching any machine, so do the certifications early — the class schedule, not the fee, is the real bottleneck.

The play: cheapest membership tier, fiber-laser certification ASAP, and treat the lab as "the place that cuts my steel" plus free networking. Everything else there is convenience, not dependency.

Crucial

the reason to join

Fiber laser (50W)

cuts thin metal sheet with precision

Makes the two parts you can't make at home: the springy steel plates the buttons press on (click feel lives here) and the toothed steel rim inside the scroll wheel that the magnetic detents grab. Feel-tuning takes many revisions — online metal-cutting services run ~$30–60 and a week per revision; here a revision is minutes and pennies of steel. This one machine is the membership.

Middle

real value, but alternatives exist

Electronics bench

soldering irons, power supplies, meters

Covers every soldering job on the build — but a $25 iron at home does the same and skips the drive. Matters as backup, and as where you happen to be during laser sessions.

3D printers

plastic parts, layer by layer

You own one. Theirs are backup capacity and possibly nicer materials for final shells and per-owner skins.

Vinyl cutter

computer-guided blade for sheet material

Cuts solder-paste stencils for board assembly and the adhesive layers under the feet. Cheap workarounds exist for both.

The people

mentors, other builders, local contacts

Folks who've seen your mistakes before — and if this becomes a business, the kind of room where local sponsor and manufacturing contacts happen. Hard to price, genuinely not nothing.

Notes

  • Never cut the skates (PTFE feet) on a laser. Lasered PTFE releases genuinely toxic fumes — most shops ban the material outright. Skates get cut on the vinyl cutter or by hand.

Still needed elsewhere

  • Fine-pitch chip assembly — home hotplate + stencil, or let the board factory place parts.
  • Radio instruments — TinySA + LiteVNA, ~$220 total, when antenna and noise work arrives.
  • Lathe work (brass flywheel) — machine shop or a business contact.
The play: the lab is nearly a complete factory for this build — the big laser cuts every layer of the pad to final size and screen printing makes the surface look like a product. Only the magnetic pattern itself happens at home.

Crucial

the reason to join

CO2 laser (60W)

cuts plastic, foam, fabric — 3×4 ft bed

The bed fits a full-size pad with room to spare. Cleanly cuts every layer of the stack — foam base, magnetic sheet, textile skin — to final dimensions, and heat-seals fabric edges as it cuts. This is the difference between craft-knife prototypes and parts that look manufactured.

Screen printing

graphics printed on fabric

Surface art on the textile skin — how the pad stops looking like a science project and starts looking like a product. Also the path to small-batch branded runs.

The play: a keyboard is mostly a case and a plate wrapped around a circuit board — and case-and-plate fabrication is exactly what this shop does best. The CNC and lasers carry the build; the electronics happen at home.

Crucial

the reason to join

ShopBot CNC

computer-guided router for wood, plastic, aluminum

The case — the heavy body everything mounts into — is the defining part of how a keyboard feels and sounds, and machining one from aluminum or hardwood is exactly this machine's job. Outsourced CNC cases are the single most expensive part of custom keyboard builds; this brings iteration in-house.

CO2 laser (60W)

cuts plastic, foam, fabric — not metal

The plate (the sheet the switches snap into) in acrylic or POM, plus the foam gasket strips that give the board its cushioned mounting, and internal dampening foam — all fast, precise laser cuts. ⚠ Skip polycarbonate in the laser — it chars and discolors; acrylic and POM cut beautifully.
