# Magneto IEM — virtual fit validation ("try-on")

Seats the generated IEM assembly in 107 real and synthetic ears and scores the
fit. Run 2026-08-30 against `cad/iem/generate.py` at commit **`8db64d7`** — the
macro-scale gyroid wing and ≥4 mm skirt contact land that came out of
`MECH_VALIDATION.md`, not the earlier `387b8eb` geometry.

## Verdict

**0 pass · 1 marginal · 106 fail out of 107 ears.**

One failure mode dominates and it is geometric, not a tolerance problem:

> **The assembly is 32.6 mm long along its nozzle axis. The ear can swallow
> about 10–18 mm of that. The faceplate therefore stands a median 13.6 mm
> proud of the tragus plane (p90 21.1 mm), on 104 of 107 ears.**

Nothing is *colliding* — hard interference now passes on **84 of 107 ears with
zero failures** (median −0.71 mm, worst −2.41 mm). The part is not too fat for
the ear. It is too **long in one direction**, because the body's long axis is
collinear with the nozzle axis, so every millimetre of driver stack is spent
along the one direction the ear has least of: concha depth.

The fix is an architecture change, not a dimension tweak — see
[CAD recommendations](#cad-recommendations).

## Method

### 1. Landmarking (`align_ear.py`)

Fully automatic; 107/107 ears landmarked with no manual override.

1. **Ear window** — anchor on the most-lateral vertex of the upper half of the
   head (a helix-rim point on every scan checked), cut an 85 × 85 × 50 mm box.
2. **Hull-relative depth map** — cast medial rays on a 0.5 mm grid against both
   the ear patch and its own convex hull, and subtract. The skull curving away
   behind the pinna lies *on* the hull and flattens to zero; the concha does not.
3. **Basin selection** — the load-bearing step. Naive "deepest point wins" picks
   the **retroauricular sulcus** (the groove behind the ear) on a large fraction
   of ears. Two gates fix it:
   - **Enclosure filter** — from 0.6 mm off the surface, cast 400 rays over the
     outward hemisphere and measure the fraction escaping 25 mm. A cavum concha
     is a bowl (escape 0.10–0.25); the sulcus opens onto free air (0.55–0.70).
     Basins above 0.42 are dropped.
   - **Bowl gate** — depth is then scored only over cells at least 0.6 × the
     basin's largest inscribed radius from its edge. Without this the cymba
     concha and crus-helicis groove win on raw depth (they run 1–6 mm deeper
     than the cavum floor) and drag the aperture 8–12 mm superior of the canal.
     A groove is deep but never wide; the cavum is both.

   Across the 107 ears, `basin_escape` came out median 0.16, max 0.33 — every
   ear cleared the 0.42 filter, so the sulcus was never selected.

### 2. Seating the IEM

The design frame assumes the nozzle axis and faceplate normal are **orthogonal**.
In a real ear the canal axis and concha-floor normal are **30–60° apart**, so
there is no single correct mapping. The pipeline therefore multi-starts: 5 nozzle
rakes × 3 rolls = 15 seeded poses, the best 4 refined with a bounded Powell search
(±10 mm, ±40°). That local search is what a wearer does when they wiggle the thing
in.

The seating cost is physical: rim in a ±0.75 mm contact band, rigid parts ≤0.3 mm
penetration (graded on the **worst** point — a mean lets a corner bury itself),
wing tip pressing ~1 mm, jacket near the concha floor, **plus two terms added
during this run** (see [corrections](#corrections-made-during-this-run)):
soft-skirt burial and faceplate protrusion.

**Sanity check on the result:** the seated nozzle ends up a median **55°** off
the inward concha normal (p10 32°, p90 75°) and the skirt rim sits a median
3.4 mm medial of the tragus plane. Both are anatomically right — the optimiser
is finding poses a real ear would accept, not numerical artifacts.

### 3. Metrics (`tryon.py`)

Sign convention: **positive = clearance, negative = inside the flesh.**

| metric | definition |
|---|---|
| `rim_cover` | fraction of 72 points on the Ø19 skirt rim with \|signed distance\| ≤ 1.5 mm |
| `rim_gap` / `rim_press` | worst positive / worst negative distance on that circle |
| `wing_tip` / `wing_mid` | median signed distance over 40 vertices each (median, so one marching-cubes spike cannot carry it) |
| `jacket_mean` / `jacket_max` | clearance of the gyroid skin that lands on the concha floor |
| `protrusion` | faceplate's furthest point past the tragus plane (normal = concha-floor outward normal) |
| `hard_min` | worst penetration of the rigid, non-pressing parts (`shell` = Ti core + faceplate + jacket body) |
| `wing_min` | worst wing penetration, reported separately and graded on retention |

Grading — overall grade is the **worst** of four axes:

| axis | pass | marginal | fail |
|---|---|---|---|
| seal | cover ≥ 0.75 | ≥ 0.50 | else |
| retention | wing_tip ∈ [−2.5, 0] | ∈ [−4, 1] | else |
| clearance | hard_min ≥ −1.0 | ≥ −2.5 | else |
| protrusion | ≤ 2 mm | ≤ 5 mm | else |

## Method validation

### Detector vs. synthetic ground truth

The synthetic ears carry exact landmarks, so the detector can be scored:

| ear | in-plane error | depth bias (medial) | nozzle-axis error |
|---|---|---|---|
| `xs_shallow` | 3.3 mm | +3.3 mm | 38° |
| `xs_deep` | 3.7 mm | +2.4 mm | 24° |
| `xl_shallow` | 3.1 mm | +7.2 mm | 24° |
| `xl_deep` | 3.5 mm | +7.1 mm | 33° |

In-plane error is a consistent ~3.4 mm. The **depth bias is systematic and
positive**: the enclosure refinement walks the pick a few mm *into* the funnel
throat, and it scales with aperture size (2.4–3.3 mm on the 7 × 4.5 mm apertures,
7.1–7.2 mm on the 18 × 14 mm ones), because a wider funnel stays enclosed for
longer.

This matters less than it looks: **the detected aperture is only the seed pose.**
The Powell search then moves ±10 mm / ±40° under a physical cost, and it is that
refined pose the metrics are read from.

### Run-to-run stability

The cost surface is built on a sampled signed-distance field and Powell finds a
*local* minimum, so results move between runs. Re-seating all 107 ears with a
different sampling seed (`--field-seed 7`):

| metric | median \|Δ\| | p90 \|Δ\| |
|---|---|---|
| `rim_cover` | 0.08 | 0.28 |
| `rim_gap` | 0.74 mm | 2.74 mm |
| `wing_tip` | 0.71 mm | 2.75 mm |
| `jacket_mean` | 0.79 mm | 2.70 mm |
| `protrusion` | 1.38 mm | 6.70 mm |
| `hard_min` | 0.32 mm | 1.91 mm |

Per-axis grade agreement between the two seeds:

| axis | same grade | fail count, seed 0 → 7 |
|---|---|---|
| protrusion | 104 / 107 | 104 → 105 |
| clearance | 84 / 107 | 0 → 2 |
| seal | 64 / 107 | 59 → 54 |
| retention | 56 / 107 | 12 → 20 |
| **overall** | **106 / 107** | **106 → 106** |

So, stated plainly:

- the overall verdict and **the protrusion conclusion are robust** — they survive
  reseeding essentially unchanged;
- population medians are trustworthy; an individual ear's millimetre is
  **±1 mm typical and worse in the tail**;
- **the seal and retention axis grades are noisy** (agreement only 64/107 and
  56/107). Both sit close to their thresholds across much of the population, so
  small pose changes flip them. The wing tip additionally rests against the
  antihelix ridge, exactly where a sampled signed-distance field's sign flips
  within ~0.3 mm. Read "most ears are marginal on seal and retention" as the
  finding; do not read the individual counts as precise.

Sampling is seeded, so a given run reproduces exactly. The tables in this report
are `--field-seed 0`.

### Data-quality flags

37 of 107 ears carry `weak` — **all** of them for `canal_run < 1.5 mm`, none for
enclosure. That is a property of the scanners, not the detector: structured light
cannot see round the first canal bend, so in both datasets the canal is a dimple,
not a tube (median `canal_run` 1.8 mm). This is why the canal probe is a
diagnostic only and never defines the nozzle axis — and why the seal numbers
below describe sealing **on the funnel entrance**, which is what this design does
anyway.

All five now show the identical signature — and it is the failure chain, not five

simultaneously opens the rim gap (seal) and lifts the wing clear of the antihelix:
every one of the five has **positive** `wing_tip`, i.e. the wing is not touching
at all. Note also that none of the five fails clearance. **Seal and retention
failures are mostly downstream of the protrusion failure**, not independent
problems — which is why the recommendations below fix protrusion first and
re-measure the rest afterwards.

## CAD recommendations

### 1. Decouple the body's long axis from the nozzle axis — *primary*

The mechanism is arithmetic. The rigid body behind the seal plane runs from
x = +4.65 to x = −18.75, i.e. **23.4 mm along the nozzle axis**. The seated
nozzle sits a median 55° off the concha normal, so the stack projected onto the
concha normal is 23.4 × cos 55° = **13.6 mm** — against a measured median
protrusion of **13.58 mm**. The model predicts the measurement to 0.02 mm.

Two levers, and only one is available:

- **Shorten the body.** To bring median protrusion to ≤2 mm needs ~12 mm off the
  along-normal stack, i.e. cutting a 23.4 mm body to about 2.4 mm. Not possible —
  the driver and mag-float carrier have to live somewhere.
- **Cant the nozzle.** Keep the body length, but angle the nozzle/carrier
  assembly **~40–50° relative to the body's long axis**, so the body lies flat in
  the concha plane (where the concha offers 11–23 mm) while the nozzle still
  reaches the canal. This is what commercial IEMs do, and it is the change the
  design's own frame assumption rules out today.

Recommended: **add a `nozzle_cant_deg` parameter to `generate.py`** (~45°,
rotating the nozzle + carrier about the design +Y relative to core/faceplate/
jacket), combined with whatever body shortening the driver stack allows.

Expected yield, from the measured distribution:

| along-normal stack removed | protrusion ≤ 2 mm | ≤ 5 mm |
|---|---|---|
| 0 mm (today) | 0.9 % | 2.8 % |
| 8 mm | 20.6 % | 46.7 % |
| 12 mm | 54.2 % | 71.0 % |
| **14 mm** | **65.4 %** | **80.4 %** |
| **16 mm** | **75.7 %** | **88.8 %** |

Target ~14–16 mm of reduction. A 45° cant on a 23.4 mm body removes
23.4 × (cos 55° − cos 85°) ≈ 11.4 mm on its own; the remainder has to come from
body length.

### 2. The Ø19 flat skirt rim cannot seal a curved concha floor

Median `rim_gap` is **+3.29 mm** while median `rim_press` is **−2.98 mm** — *on
the same ear*. One side of the rim is buried ~3 mm while the opposite side floats
~3.3 mm off the surface. That is a **tilt/curvature mismatch**, not a diameter
error: a flat Ø19 circle laid on a curved bowl.

Total rim excursion required: median **6.4 mm**, p90 8.3 mm, max 11.7 mm. 98 % of
ears need more than 3 mm; 80 % need more than 5 mm. The reworked skirt — a 4.5 mm
conical contact land on a 0.25 mm wall with a 0.2 × 0.6 mm compliance groove — is
a real improvement on the old 0.35 mm lip, and it moved seal passes from 2 to 4.
It still does not have 6 mm of travel.

Options, in order of preference:

1. **Shrink the rim** to Ø14–15 mm so it lands *inside* the cavum floor rather
   than spanning the bowl onto its walls. Excursion falls roughly with the square
   of the radius spanned.
2. **Add axial compliance** — a bellows/rolling-diaphragm neck with ≥6 mm of
   travel, rather than relying on skirt bending.
3. **Contour the rim** to the mean concha-floor curvature instead of a flat
   circle, which addresses the tilt term directly.

Re-measure after change 1 — a body that no longer stands proud will re-seat the
rim, and some of this gap is downstream of the protrusion problem.

### 3. Wing reach — re-evaluate after 1, do not fix in isolation

`wing_tip` median is **−0.19 mm** against a −0.5 to −2.0 mm target: only 28 % of
ears are in band and **44 % have the wing not touching at all** (positive
distance). The obvious read is "extend the wing ~1–1.5 mm", but the wing is
lifting largely *because the shell is standing out of the bowl*. Fix the
protrusion first, then re-run; the retention axis is also the least stable metric
here (see [stability](#run-to-run-stability)).

Note the macro-scale gyroid wing is already reaching 2 mm further than its
predecessor (tip at y = 21.2 vs 19.2 mm) and retention still only moved from 51
to 54 passes — more reach alone is not closing this.

### 4. Jacket is bridging, not bedding

`jacket_mean` median **4.13 mm**, with 73 % of ears above 3 mm. The gyroid skin
is riding on the bowl rim instead of bedding into the concha floor — again a
consequence of the body sitting proud, and expected to resolve with change 1.

### Not a problem

**Clearance.** 84 pass / 23 marginal / **0 fail**, median −0.71 mm, worst
−2.41 mm across all 107 ears. There is no hard-interference blocker anywhere in
the population, and the macro-scale wing rework improved this axis outright (3
fails → 0 against the previous geometry). Whatever else is wrong, the part is not
too bulky for the ear — which is what makes the cant fix in change 1 plausible
rather than wishful.

## Corrections made during this run

Two bugs in the paused pipeline were found and fixed; both changed the reported
numbers materially, so they are recorded here rather than buried in the diff.

1. **The seating optimiser was driving the skirt into the skull.** `c_rim` scores
   `|distance|`, so a rim 0.5 mm *inside* the flesh scored as well as one touching
   it, and nothing opposed burial. The optimiser bought rim coverage by ramming
   the whole Ø19 skirt down the canal — 13 to 20 mm medial of the tragus plane on
   the synthetic corners, which is inside the head, and impossible for a Ø19 rim
   against an aperture at most 18 × 14 mm. Added `c_soft` (skirt burial, ~0.75 mm
   mean / 1.5 mm worst-point allowance for silicone) and `c_prot` (faceplate
   protrusion), so the reported pose is one a wearer would actually adopt.

2. **The clearance axis contradicted the retention axis.** `hard_min` was computed
   over `rigid`, which *includes* the wing — but the wing is designed to press
   −0.5 to −2.0 mm into the antihelix, so an on-target wing forced `hard_min ≤ −2`
   and could never score better than "marginal" on clearance. Clearance is now
   graded on `shell` (rigid minus wing), matching what the seating search
   penalises; the wing's own worst penetration is reported as `wing_min` and
   graded on retention.

Also: seeded the surface sampling (results were drifting ~0.1 mm between runs),
raised the Powell refinement from the best 2 starts to the best 4, decimated the
faceplate point set (22 k → 300; it was the most expensive term in the search),
synced `SKIRT_RIM_X`/`SKIRT_RIM_R` to the reworked skirt (rim Ø19.0 exactly,
0.25 mm land wall), and added `--skip-existing` / `--reseat` so cost-function
changes do not require re-cutting every head mesh.

## Known limitations

- **Right ears only.** The master STLs are cut for the right ear; the pipeline
  supports `--side left` but it was not run.
- **Scanned canals are dimples, not tubes** (median `canal_run` 1.8 mm). Valid
  for this design, which seals on the funnel entrance, but this dataset cannot
  validate anything that enters the canal — including the three nozzle inserts.
- **The synthetic XL corners have an unrealistically wide funnel mouth** (built at
  2× aperture, so 36 × 28 mm), large enough to swallow the Ø19 skirt. Their seal
  numbers are optimistic; the 103 real ears carry the conclusion.
- **Both scan populations are European-recruited university cohorts.** The four
  synthetic corners are the only coverage of the envelope tails.
- **Static geometry only.** No skin deformation, no silicone FEA, no insertion
  path, no jaw movement. Soft-part compliance is modelled as fixed millimetre
  allowances, not simulated. For the mechanical side see `MECH_VALIDATION.md`.

## Reproducing

```bash
cd cad/iem && python -m venv .venv && .venv/bin/pip install -r requirements.txt
.venv/bin/python fetch_ears.py --source all --n 45     # ~1.7 GB, gitignored
.venv/bin/python make_synthetic_ear.py
.venv/bin/python align_ear.py --dataset sonicom   --qc-png --skip-existing
.venv/bin/python align_ear.py --dataset hutubs    --qc-png --skip-existing
.venv/bin/python align_ear.py --dataset synthetic --qc-png --skip-existing
.venv/bin/python tryon.py
```

`align_ear.py --reseat` re-runs only the seating search over already-landmarked
ears (~10 min for 107 at `--jobs 8`), which is what to use when tuning the cost.
`--qc-png` writes a per-ear depth map with the detected aperture and tragus
marked; `--landmarks FILE` overrides a bad pick by hand.
